## The problem

Game wikis are huge, slow to search mid-session, and go stale. The idea: a
Discord bot that answers Helldivers 2 intel questions (units, counters,
loadouts, biomes, live war status) from a local cache that refreshes itself —
with a personality players actually enjoy talking to.

## What I built

A Go-only bot (no Python runtime) roleplaying a defected informant. Questions
flow through a think-act loop of up to 10 tool rounds over exact-match Mongo
intel tools — unit/gear/world lookups, generic war summary, named-planet
status, wiki search — defined once and shared between the relay client and an
MCP stdio server. Retrieval is Mongo exact-match with cited context (collated
indexes, alias handling); on a local miss it falls back to live wiki lookup
with source links. Live war status comes from the Training Manual API with a
staleness guard, answering generic "how goes the war" questions without
needing a planet name. Gear shelves sync daily from a public catalog snapshot
(ETag-conditional, cached in Mongo); units and world are crawled politely
from the wiki, and a self-feeding watcher polls the wiki RecentChanges feed
every 5 minutes, routes unseen pages to a shelf, and re-parses only changed
pages.

The craft is in the guardrails: allowlist + mention gating with auto-threads,
answers truncated to Discord's 2000-rune limit, the persona compiled into the
binary with serial numbers redacted and every transmission ending in `Over.`,
and failure voiced in character — a 429 gets a "busy spreading democracy"
reply with a liberation ETA, never silence. Client-side token-bucket rate
limiting backs that up, with `/status` (health + relay probe) and `/cooldown`
(quota inquiry) ops commands and a typing indicator while the model works. Static binaries ship in a
distroless image under Podman Compose (bot, watcher, catalog sync, crawl, Mongo), seeded by a
one-shot ingest from checked-in intel data covering units, gear, and world shelves.

## What it taught me

Constraints make bots: capped tool rounds, clamped result counts, and canned
failure modes turn an LLM integration from a demo into something that behaves
in production. And embedding the persona at compile time means the character
survives every redeploy bit-for-bit.
