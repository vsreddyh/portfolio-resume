## The problem

Paper registers and proxy attendance don't scale for large batches. The goal:
walk up, enter a roll number, show your face, and get a trustworthy check-in —
with admins able to see who was actually present, per class, per day.

## What I built

Three services behind two frontends. The Attendance app takes a roll number
plus a live photo (with a custom on-screen virtual keyboard for touchscreens)
and POSTs to Express, which pulls the enrolled photo from a GridFS bucket and
forwards both images to a Flask microservice. Flask runs the `face_recognition`
pipeline — dlib 128-d embedding, Euclidean distance, 0.6 threshold, first face
only — and returns the match distance. On match, Express writes the record.

The rules engine is where the domain lives: check-in before 12:00, checkout
only after a 6-hour gap, one in/out pair per day, and attendance percentage
computed against active class dates. The Admin app manages the hierarchy
(batch → class → student) with cascade deletes wired as Mongoose hooks, so
removing a batch can't orphan records. Auth is session-cookie + bcrypt, and
the whole Attendance frontend ships wrapped as a Linux Electron app for kiosk
hardware.

## On the accuracy number

Matching runs on dlib's ResNet embedding (99.38% on LFW, per the model
publisher). That's the library's benchmark, not an in-repo evaluation — the
right way to read it is "proven model, threshold tuned for the deployment,"
not a claim about our hallways.
